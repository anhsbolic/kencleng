# Kencleng Brand + Product UI Design Brief

> Status: Canonical
> Selected direction: **Sunlit Editorial**
> Core thesis: **Evidence-Led Optimism**
> Human approval: Approved 2026-09-15

## 1. Design Thesis

Kencleng should feel like a thoughtful, transparent, optimistic donation platform where trust comes from visible evidence, clear progress, respectful storytelling, and consistent truthfulness — not emotional pressure or symbolic trust theatre.

Short form:

> **Evidence-Led Optimism**

Supporting phrase:

> **Hope, structured by evidence.**

The intended experience sequence is:

```text
evidence
→ confidence
→ optimism
→ action
```

Kencleng should help users understand enough to trust their decision before asking them to act.

## 2. Brand Attributes

### Thoughtful
Information is considered, ordered, and contextual. The product does not rush users through emotionally sensitive decisions.

### Candid
Unknown, pending, delayed, changed, and incomplete states are allowed to remain visible. The platform avoids euphemism and overclaim.

### Composed
Kencleng remains calm even when content is emotionally difficult. Sensitive campaign content does not require visual drama.

### Human
Real people and real contexts matter. The product preserves dignity and avoids turning people into conversion assets.

### Optimistic
Kencleng shows possibility and progress without promising outcomes it cannot prove.

### Refined but Accessible
The product should feel intentional and high-quality without becoming exclusive, premium-financial, or intimidating.

## 3. Operational Design Principles

### Confidence Before Conversion
Users should receive enough context to make an informed decision before being pushed toward donation. CTA clarity is important; CTA pressure is not.

### Facts First, Story With Them
Campaign content should behave as **facts that have a story**, not story decorated with selective facts.

### Dignity Over Pity
Hardship may be shown. Suffering must not become the primary persuasive device. Imagery and copy must preserve dignity, privacy, agency, and context.

### Optimism Must Remain Evidence-Aware
Hope is expressed through possibility, participation, visible progress, reported milestones, and constructive next steps. Hope is not permission to imply certainty.

### Unknown Is a Valid State
The system must be comfortable showing information that is unavailable, in progress, not yet reported, changed, delayed, or under review.

### Trust Comes From Structure
Trust should emerge through hierarchy, chronology, provenance, consistent language, factual separation, and predictable behavior. Badge and color are secondary.

### Progress Is Evidence, Not Gamification
Progress should orient and inform. It must not turn generosity into scoring. Funding, operational execution, and reported outcomes must remain conceptually distinct.

### Public Can Be Expressive; Product Must Stay Disciplined
Public surfaces can carry more editorial expression. Authenticated operational surfaces must preserve clarity, scanability, state legibility, and restraint.

## 4. Visual Language

### Typography Direction
Use a mixed typography model:

- expressive human/editorial display typography for public headings, campaign editorial moments, selected milestones, and key brand statements;
- calm, highly readable typography for forms, navigation, metadata, status, body copy, data, and operational surfaces.

Exact typefaces remain intentionally open until the concrete production visual system is derived.

### Color Strategy
Selected territory: **Sunlit Editorial**.

Preferred posture:

- warm ivory / paper-like neutral base;
- clear bright editorial yellow used with restraint;
- mature warm ink / dark neutral;
- selective secondary accent.

The visual system should remain light and avoid excessive saturation. Yellow is brand energy and optimism; it is not trust proof, a universal success state, or a body-text color.

Exact color values remain intentionally open.

### Surface / Shape Language
Use mixed editorial surfaces with a structured foundation, selective softness, varied grouping, spacing-led hierarchy, tonal sections, selective borders/rules, and selective card containers.

Avoid endless rounded cards, excessive floating surfaces, glassmorphism, decorative blobs, sterile flat minimalism, and generic AI-startup aesthetics.

### Composition
Public experience should use editorial composition rather than generic SaaS composition.

Preferred public hierarchy:

- real imagery;
- campaign purpose;
- transparency / trust explanation;
- steward;
- factual progress;
- action.

Product experience should prioritize:

- current state;
- chronology;
- next expected state;
- source/provenance;
- milestone evidence;
- related action.

## 5. Product UI Language

### Action Hierarchy
Primary actions should be visible, direct, calm, and non-coercive. Donation CTA may be prominent but must not use artificial urgency, guilt, exaggerated scarcity, or manipulative framing.

### Forms
Forms should feel clear, respectful, forgiving, low-friction, and explicit about consequences. Sensitive actions prioritize comprehension over speed.

### Cards / Content
Campaign cards should use a hybrid editorial model balancing real image, campaign purpose, steward/context, and factual progress. They must not become image-heavy marketplace tiles.

### Status / Data Presentation
Status should use plain language, hierarchy, timestamp/context where relevant, provenance, and next-step clarity. Do not depend on color, icon, or badge alone.

### Trust Cues
Trust cues should be distributed across the experience. Important examples include steward identity, campaign state, progress, chronology, update source, separation between platform fact and organizer report, and visible pending states.

A dedicated transparency area may exist, but trust cannot be hidden only there.

### Empty / Loading / Error
Empty states may use gentle humanity and illustration where appropriate. Loading should communicate system progress simply. Error states should remain calm and specific; playful illustration is inappropriate around money, privacy, access, campaign state, or trust failures.

### Responsive Behavior
Responsive adaptation must preserve information priority, visual dignity, trust hierarchy, image context, and action clarity. Public surfaces may simplify composition without hiding important trust context. Authenticated surfaces prioritize reading order over decorative fidelity.

Exact breakpoints are an engineering freedom.

### Motion
Motion should feel calm, purposeful, orienting, and human. Use it for state changes, progress, timeline progression, completion, disclosure, and orientation. Avoid default confetti, score-like progress, exaggerated spring behavior, and novelty motion.

## 6. Trust Expression

Trust is not a visual style. It is a system behavior.

Users should quickly understand:

- what matters;
- what is known;
- who is responsible;
- what has happened;
- what comes next.

The system should conceptually distinguish:

- platform fact;
- organizer-provided information;
- organizer report;
- system status;
- pending information;
- reported outcome.

Preferred copy style is plain, respectful, candid, specific, and non-judgmental.

Avoid exaggerated certainty, impact guarantees, euphemistic hiding of negative states, and guilt-based persuasion.

## 7. Content and Asset Strategy

### Photography
Direction: **editorial documentary**.

Photography should be real, contextual, dignified, warm without artificial happiness, capable of showing difficult reality, and selective about privacy.

Avoid pity-driven framing, exaggerated suffering, generic stock-photo optimism, and staged charity imagery.

### Campaign Imagery
Campaign imagery belongs to campaign reality. Kencleng provides framing, not fictional enhancement. Images must not imply beneficiary identity, impact, distribution, or verification unless evidence supports it.

### Illustration
Direction: **mature human editorial illustration with supporting abstract explanatory graphics**.

Illustration may support process explanation, trust education, privacy-sensitive content, onboarding, empty states, brand storytelling, and key art. Illustration must not masquerade as real-world campaign evidence.

### Iconography
Iconography should be simple, mature, humanized, clear, and non-corporate. Avoid excessive shield/checkmark vocabulary.

### Wordmark / Logo
The wordmark may become distinctive, but product identity must not depend on the word “Kencleng” or literal kencleng/celengan metaphors. The system should survive a future naming change.

## 8. Representative Public Experience

The approved public reference proves the direction for acquisition, public trust, storytelling, and campaign discovery.

Representative characteristics:

- large real campaign imagery;
- expressive editorial headline;
- transparency/trust explanation visible early;
- campaign story present but not dominant;
- steward and progress visible;
- CTA clear but restrained;
- editorial yellow used selectively;
- supporting human illustration / graphic notes;
- campaign presentation as editorial content rather than marketplace inventory.

Public experience should feel bright, thoughtful, hopeful, transparent, editorial, and human.

## 9. Representative Product Experience

The approved donor post-donation reference proves that the language can remain credible as real product UI.

Primary product concept: **Evidence Journal**.

A donor should be able to understand:

- donation received;
- campaign progress;
- operational updates;
- reports;
- what remains pending;
- what outcomes have been reported.

The journal is factual first and human second.

## 10. Progress as Evidence

This is a signature product-language principle.

### Funding Progress
How much funding has been collected.

### Operational Progress
What execution step has happened.

### Reported Outcome
What result has been reported.

Do not merge these into one universal progress bar.

A full funding bar does not mean operational completion. Operational completion does not automatically prove impact. Reported impact must remain labeled according to its source and evidence status.

## 11. Rejected Directions

### Quiet Stewardship
Rejected as primary because it over-optimized institutional credibility and risked making Kencleng cold, administrative, distant, and fintech-like.

### Calm Evidence
Not selected as primary because it risked excessive restraint, low warmth, and generic editorial minimalism. Its trust discipline was retained.

### Hope in Motion
Its optimistic character became the emotional foundation, but the direction was not used directly because optimism without evidence discipline could drift toward impact marketing, optimism-washing, or gamified progress.

### Human Rose
Rejected as selected palette territory because it risked lifestyle/wellness character and excessive emotionality.

### Violet Bloom
Rejected as selected palette territory because it risked an AI/startup feel, gradient temptation, and weaker fit with grounded documentary imagery.

## 13. Engineering Invariants

When translated into frontend, engineering must preserve:

- Evidence-Led Optimism as the core experience thesis;
- public surfaces as editorial and human rather than generic SaaS;
- disciplined and clear product surfaces;
- trust through transparent information structure rather than badge theatre;
- distinct truth classes for campaign facts, organizer reports, system states, and reported outcomes;
- visible unknown/pending information when relevant;
- clear CTA without manipulative urgency;
- non-gamified donation progress;
- separation of funding progress, operational progress, and reported outcome;
- dignified campaign imagery that does not imply unproven facts;
- real-world grounding in photography;
- illustration never masquerading as evidence;
- optimism that does not erase difficult reality;
- information hierarchy that does not rely solely on color;
- one recognizable brand across public and authenticated surfaces despite different expressive intensity;
- viability if the product name changes in the future.
