## 1. Core distinctions

- **Identity is not authority.** A Registered User is an authenticated Kencleng account identity; account creation or sign-in does not confer an organizational mandate.
- **Application relationship is not organizational control.** A person may initiate a claim about an organization and manage their own application within a bounded scope without being that organization's representative, Staff, or Owner.
- **Organizational authority is action- and scope-specific.** A valid mandate to submit materials does not automatically authorize organizational commitments or appointment of a First Owner.
- **Assessed facts are not submitted claims.** Documents, references, identity statements, mandates, and acceptance claims require proportionate evidence review before Kencleng recognizes their consequential meaning.
- **Organization Eligibility is distinct from individual authority.** Legal identity, organizational eligibility, individual mandates, and authorized application access are different questions. Losing one person's mandate does not erase the organization or its history.
- **One recognized organization identity per underlying legal entity** is the working invariant. Multiple people may claim or apply for that entity; priority of submission never proves control.

## 2. Reusable Product actors and responsibilities

| Actor / concept | Product meaning | Consequential boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered User | A Kencleng account identity with a valid authenticated context for application actions | Not an automatic organization member or global privileged user |
| Applicant | A user with a bounded, legitimate relationship to a particular application | Can initiate, provide information, and follow their application as permitted; not automatically an Authorized Representative, Owner, or Staff |
| Authorized Representative | A person or governing organ with sufficiently evidenced organizational authority for a specific act | May bind the organization or delegate only within the actual mandate and its validity; does not necessarily require a Kencleng account solely to provide off-platform evidence of authorization |
| First Owner Candidate | Proposed Kencleng user to receive initial organizational management authority | No Owner rights arise from nomination or self-assertion alone |
| Recognized First Owner | Valid Kencleng user with sufficient organizational mandate, explicit informed acceptance, and legitimate Kencleng recognition | Initial Owner rights take effect only with the applicable initial eligibility approval; precise permission scope remains to be defined for affected operations |
| Staff | Organizational actor whose responsibilities differ from Owner | No automatic grant from application submission; detailed Staff delegation is outside PO-01 |
| Reviewer | Kencleng-authorized responsibility to examine evidence, record findings, request permissible clarification, and make a reasoned recommendation | Must not assess a case with a disqualifying conflict of interest; review is not itself final eligibility approval |
| Decision-Maker | Kencleng-authorized responsibility to decide an initial eligibility outcome | Decision must satisfy policy criteria, evidence sufficiency, applicable authority, conflict safeguards, and auditable rationale |
| Authorized Platform Operator | Kencleng responsibility for legitimate assignment/routing/escalation when applicable | Label does not imply unrestricted administrator rights or authorization to override evidentiary requirements |
| Organization | Underlying legal entity to which eligibility, accepted obligations, and consequential decisions relate | Distinct from Applicant accounts and from Campaigns |
| Visitor / Donor | Downstream actors affected by honest organization and Campaign claims | Organization approval alone does not establish Campaign approval, donation eligibility, settlement, use-of-funds verification, or real-world impact |

These are Product responsibilities and relationships, **not** a required number of distinct human staff, backend roles, database tables, screens, or permission strings.

## 3. First Owner mandate recognition — PO-01 working decision

A First Owner may be someone other than the organization's legal chair/management, provided the appointing party actually has the power to delegate organizational management authority of the necessary scope.

Three distinct consequential acts must be independently supportable:

1. **Mandate granted:** a legitimately authorized organizational party appoints the candidate with authority sufficient for Kencleng organizational management. Consider source, scope, validity, and conflicting/revoked mandates.
2. **Mandate accepted:** the specific First Owner Candidate, linked to a valid Kencleng user identity, explicitly and knowingly accepts the role/responsibilities.
3. **Mandate recognized:** Kencleng evaluates traceable supporting evidence and legitimizes recognition through authorized human assessment/decision.

A person providing external evidence of authority need not automatically become a Kencleng user. Any person performing protected in-product actions must meet applicable identity and authorization requirements. Candidate acceptance does not substitute for separate **authorized organizational acceptance of accountability obligations**, even if one qualified person performs both acts.

First Owner recognition cannot be treated as an unrestricted grant to publish Campaigns, receive donations, move funds, replace Owners unilaterally, or bypass independent downstream decisions.

## 4. Kencleng decision authority and accountability — PO-01 working decision

Initial Organization Eligibility uses **manual, human-led assessment and approval** by authorized Kencleng actors. Evidence completeness, document submission, or any auxiliary automated check cannot grant eligibility automatically.

Reviewer and Decision-Maker are distinguishable responsibilities; for routine cases, the same authorized person may carry both when there is no disqualifying conflict of interest. Materially contradictory mandates, credible takeover risk, or material decision-authority/independence concerns require holding the affected decision and authorized, independent escalation. Escalation is **not** a waiver of substantive evidence requirements. A reviewer with a disqualifying conflict cannot assess that case.

The record must allow legitimate reconstruction of: application and legal entity; assigned examiner and decision actor with relevant authority; examined claims/evidence and provenance; substantive findings and limitations; decision criteria and rationale; chronology of material acts and effective consequences; and subsequent corrections/history. No silent overwrite of consequential decision history. Disclosure is limited by legitimate need and privacy rights.

Human Product decision authority over these rules is separate from operational Kencleng authority to decide individual organization applications.

## 5. Access, competing applications, and privacy

- Applicant access follows the specific application relationship and necessary permissions, not automatic access to organization-wide evidence, disputed authority documents, or another applicant's file.
- If several applicants assert relationships to the same legal entity, Kencleng must avoid duplicating recognized organizational identity or giving priority-based ownership. Conflicting, material authority claims block affected rights pending adequate assessment.
- Every actor gets a truthful explanation of their own permissible next steps without unauthorized disclosure of others' personal data, mandates, or confidential evidence.
- Account identity and application-specific sensitive evidence have different data purposes; required collection, public disclosure, access, retention/deletion, and legal/privacy basis remain bounded Product/privacy work, not invented universal consent or mandatory personal-document collection.

## 6. Not settled; cannot be inferred from this model

- Exact First Owner management actions/limits, number of Owners, later Owner delegation/replacement and Staff permissions.
- Technical role/permission inventory, storage model, authentication, audit implementation, and admin tooling.
- Detailed dispute resolution and appeal process, platform assignment policy, material escalation thresholds beyond representative scenarios, and exceptional separation-of-duties requirements.
- Legal sufficiency and privacy/legal clearance of any particular identity/mandate document.
- Organization's ongoing eligibility lifecycle after initial approval.
